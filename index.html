eMudhra G3 CA compromise
Exposure check

Eight eMudhra G3 intermediate CAs have private keys anyone can compute, and Sectigo cross-signed all eight with the same keys [R001, R002]. Browsers block them [R026, R027, R035, R033]. Your OS packages, runtimes, language packages, JDKs, apps and container images carry their own trust stores and need their own updates [R038, R039]. Start with the checks.

What happened

What it is
  • Eight eMudhra G3 intermediates have private keys anyone can compute [R001]. Their P-384 keys are whole numbers below 256, namely 4, 56, 66, 115, 155, 156, 230 and 237 [R001].
  • Six are TLS CAs and two are code-signing CAs [R001]. The TLS CAs sit under emSign Root TLS CA - G3 and the code-signing CAs under emSign Root CS CA - G3 [R001]. Browsers trust the TLS CAs through emSign ECC Root CA - G3, which cross-signs emSign Root TLS CA - G3 [R005].
  • Sectigo's eight cross-signs reuse the same keys, so 16 certificates are affected [R002]. Sectigo issued them under its AAA Certificate Services root, dated 14 May 2025 [R002]. The repo's rules/iocs.csv lists all 16 with serials and SHA-256 fingerprints [R003].
  • A TLS key lets anyone forge a certificate for any website [R004]. Any client that still trusts the chain accepts the forgery, which opens its traffic to interception [R004]. The two code-signing keys could make malware look like it comes from a trusted publisher on Windows [R004].
  • Chrome's paranoid_crypto check found no other weak CA keys [R036]. It checked the Chrome-trusted CA certificates in its monitoring data and in CCADB within an hour of the report [R036].
Cause
  • A key ceremony rehearsal left two key objects per CA [R008]. At the 30 April 2025 ceremony, eMudhra's script generated each B and C series key, then wrapped, unwrapped and re-imported it [R008]. The CA certificates were issued over the re-imported key objects rather than the originals [R008].
  • Only the P-384 keys of series B and C are affected [R001]. The eight came from a batch of 24 intermediates made that day [R001]. The affected keys were generated on Utimaco HSMs, while series A and A-1 were generated on Entrust nShield HSMs and skipped the rehearsal [R008].
  • eMudhra has given two accounts of why the weak keys signed the CSRs [R009]. On 19 October it said staff picked them by mistake because the key labels were similar [R009]. On 28 October it said the ceremony script itself specified the re-imported keys [R009].
Timeline
  • 17 October: found, reported and blocked [R010]. A researcher scanning certificates disclosed in CCADB found the weak keys at 16:27 UTC and reported them to Apple, Chrome, Microsoft, Mozilla and eMudhra at 18:05 UTC [R010]. Chrome's CRLSet blocked the keys at 19:45 UTC [R026]. eMudhra acknowledged at about 21:28 UTC and said EM DV TLS CA - G3C had been live, and Mozilla recovered the keys on its own at 21:48 UTC [R010].
  • 17 October: Firefox OneCRL [R027]. The block reached all Firefox users on standard settings by 22:59 UTC [R027]. The day is disputed, because the NSS 3.131.1 announcement relayed on oss-security says OneCRL went out on Saturday 19th, while Mozilla's blog and the bug say 17 October [R027].
  • 18 October: eMudhra revoked the eight [R011]. It revoked them with reason cACompromise between 09:24 and 09:38 UTC [R011]. Mozilla opened Bug 2083449 at 12:06 UTC [R012].
  • 19 October: Sectigo revoked the cross-signs [R011]. Its CRL dates the revocation 10:35:36 UTC, and Sectigo also revoked its other eMudhra cross-signs and ended the relationship [R011]. Mozilla warned downstream trust store maintainers the same day [R012]. Android got the Chrome block through Google Play services around 22:00 UTC [R026].
  • 20 October: root removal decided [R028]. Mozilla chose emergency removal of emSign ECC Root CA - G3, and Chrome committed to the same [R028]. Mozilla published its blog post on the incident [R012]. eMudhra confirmed at 16:27 UTC that it had stopped all G3 issuance [R016].
Root programs
  • Chrome removed emSign ECC Root CA - G3 in Chrome Root Store v43 [R031]. Version 42 still has it [R031]. Google expected v43 on all devices on the morning of 27 October (Pacific time), and no source read confirms it shipped [R031]. The 17 October CRLSet blocks by public key, so it also covers the Sectigo cross-signs [R026].
  • Mozilla removed the root in NSS 3.131.1 on 20 October [R029]. The release also removes emSign Root CA - C1 and emSign ECC Root CA - C3 [R029]. Firefox 158.0.1, ESR 153.5.1 and ESR 115.43.1 shipped the same day with the block built in, for users whose settings keep OneCRL from updating [R030]. Whether ESR 115.43.1 is fixed is disputed [R030].
  • Microsoft removed six emSign G3 roots and AAA Certificate Services [R034]. Windows dropped them in a 26 October out-of-band update (KB5134133, KB5134137), and Edge and WebView2 on 27 October in PKI Metadata 72.0.0.0 [R034]. On 20 October the Windows Disallowed CTL had already blocked the eight keys, and Edge CRLSet 2026.10.20 the TLS keys [R033].
  • Apple blocks the 12 TLS certificates and the G3 ECC root [R035]. The 12 are the six eMudhra TLS CAs and their six Sectigo cross-signs, and the root block started on 23 October [R035]. Apple dates the first block to 19 October, while the community tracker says 22 October [R035]. Command-line tools that read /etc/ssl/cert.pem skip Apple's block [R044].
  • No root program has removed emSign Root CA - G1 [R032]. Chrome Root Store v43 and NSS master still carry it, Mozilla said on 20 October it was not removing it at that time, and Microsoft is evaluating the remaining eMudhra roots [R032]. OpenJDK's interim fix deletes the G1 alias anyway [R037].
eMudhra since
  • eMudhra is leaving the TLS business [R015]. Its board decided on 22 October to leave the global SSL/TLS certificate market, and its G1 RSA roots keep serving customers until the transition ends [R015].
  • 25,058 G3 certificates were still valid on 28 October [R016]. All sit under two InCommon CAs, and eMudhra does not force their replacement [R016]. US ACME subscribers move to a hierarchy under emSign Root CA - G1, and non-US subscribers are re-issued through Sectigo under its R46 or E46 roots [R016]. eMudhra expects the non-US re-issuance to finish by 15 November and has no date for moving US subscribers off G1 [R016].
  • The root-cause report is still owed [R018]. eMudhra promised a full report through CCADB but had filed none on Bug 2083449 by 02:21 UTC on 31 October [R018]. It says it appointed KPMG as independent reviewer on 30 October, a claim that rests only on eMudhra's own status page [R017].
Misuse
  • No source reports attacks on users [R014]. None of the root program or CA statements read mentions misuse of the keys [R014]. eMudhra says it issued no end-entity certificates from the eight CAs and put only EM DV TLS CA - G3C into production [R014].
  • Nothing was in CT before Chrome's block [R046]. Google says no TLS certificate under the six TLS CAs was logged to CT before then, so clients that enforce CT could not have been attacked [R046]. Whether anyone used the keys against clients that don't enforce CT before disclosure is unknown [R014].
  • A count of 107,107 post-disclosure CT certificates is unconfirmed [R013]. One commenter on Bug 2083449 counted that many certificates under six of the keys in e-Szignó's CT logs, logged on 20 and 21 October, and called 106,050 of them proof-of-concept certificates for google[.]com [R013]. Nobody has replicated the count [R013].
CVE IDs
  • The compromise has no CVE of its own [R019]. Red Hat says it is not a software vulnerability, and the CVEs track downstream trust store updates [R019]. CVE-2026-111944 has no CVE record [R019].
  • CVE-2026-114820 and CVE-2026-91403 are Oracle CNA records [R022]. CVE-2026-114820 covers Java SE and GraalVM updates that remove the eMudhra roots [R022]. CVE-2026-91403 covers Java updates that distrust the intermediates [R023]. OpenJDK's own advisory reuses CVE-2026-114820, with issue JDK-8395690 as its fix marker [R022].
  • Go and Red Hat have their own CVEs [R020]. CVE-2026-111943 (Go CNA) adds a crypto/x509 denylist, fixed in Go 1.26.10 and 1.27.3 [R020]. CVE-2026-114406 (Red Hat) covers the RHEL ca-certificates package [R021].
  • Firefox, Electron and Perl have CVEs too [R030]. CVE-2026-113470 (Mozilla) covers the Firefox and Thunderbird block lists [R030]. CVE-2026-115570 (GitHub, GHSA-jr5w-w6x2-84p9) covers Electron before 42.11.16, 43.8.1, 44.9.1 and 45.0.0-beta.6 [R025]. Every fixed Electron release checked still carries the G3 root, because the fix blocks the intermediates and leaves the root in place [R271]. CVE-2026-113812 (CPANSec) covers Perl Mozilla::CA before 20261020 [R024].

Who is exposed

Update everything that carries its own trust store [R038]. Browsers on default settings are covered by pushed blocks and root store changes [R026, R027, R035, R033]. OS bundles, language packages, JDK cacerts, runtimes, Electron apps and container images each need their own update [R038, R039, R025].

  • Browsers on default settings are covered [R026]. Chrome's CRLSet reached users on 17 October [R026]. Mozilla says Firefox's OneCRL block did too, though its NSS 3.131.1 announcement says 19 October [R027]. Apple, Windows and Edge pushed their own blocks between 19 and 22 October [R035, R033]. A new Firefox ESR 115 profile can still trust the chains until OneCRL loads after 20 idle seconds [R030].
  • Apps that ship their own CA bundle need their own update, even on a patched Windows host [R038]. Microsoft says software that doesn't reliably check revocation or its disallowed list can still accept the chains if it keeps or restores trust in the removed roots [R109]. macOS command-line tools that read /etc/ssl/cert.pem, including curl, git, python3, ruby, perl and openssl, skip Apple's block and still accept the chains [R044]. A container trusts the CA bundle inside its image, so a patched host does not protect it [R038].
  • Runtimes and JDKs carry their own copy [R039]. Node.js bundles a Mozilla snapshot fixed at release time, so only a Node.js upgrade changes it [R039]. A JDK update fixes only its bundled cacerts, and an app's own JKS or PKCS12 truststore needs the roots removed from that file [R039]. Electron's advisory gives 42.11.16, 43.8.1, 44.9.1 and 45.0.0-beta.6 as the first releases that block the intermediates in its Chromium networking and Node.js TLS [R025]. Each Electron app needs its own release on one of them [R272]. Fixed Electron builds still contain the G3 root, so check each app's Electron version to tell fixed from unfixed [R271].
  • A store with AAA Certificate Services and no G3 root can still trust a forged chain [R002]. Sectigo cross-signed all eight compromised intermediates under its AAA Certificate Services root, and each cross-sign reuses the same weak key [R002]. A forged leaf can chain through those cross-signed copies to AAA, so removing emSign ECC Root CA - G3 alone does not block it [R040]. Before the incident Apple and Microsoft still trusted AAA, while Mozilla and Chrome had removed it [R007].
  • Microsoft removed AAA [R034]. Windows dropped it on 26 October, and Edge and WebView2 on 27 October [R034]. The 20 October Disallowed CTL block matches the keys, so it covers the cross-signs too [R033].
  • Mozilla trusts AAA for email only [R041]. NSS and Firefox carry the root without TLS trust [R041]. Mozilla left the cross-signs out of OneCRL, saying the root is not in its root store [R041].
  • Apple blocks the six TLS cross-signs [R035]. They are among the 12 TLS certificates Apple blocks [R035]. In /etc/ssl/cert.pem they still verify through AAA, so Apple's interim steps offer removing AAA as well as G3 [R040].
  • Chrome's store holds no AAA root [R042]. Chrome Root Store v41, v42 and v43 do not contain it [R042]. Chrome's CRLSet blocks by public key, so it covers the cross-signs anyway [R026].
  • Sectigo revoked the cross-signs on 19 October [R011]. Its CRL dates the revocation 10:35:36 UTC [R011]. Clients that skip revocation checks, such as the macOS tools that read /etc/ssl/cert.pem, still accept the chains [R044].
  • Fixes that block by public key also catch the cross-signs [R043]. Go's crypto/x509 denylist, rustls-webpki 0.103.16 and CPython's ssl block match the compromised keys [R043]. So do Chrome's CRLSet and the Windows Disallowed CTL [R026, R033]. A fix that only removes the G3 root leaves the AAA path open wherever AAA is still trusted [R040].
  • Common tools could not fetch eMudhra's revocation data until 26 October [R045]. eMudhra's firewall refused HTTP CRL and OCSP requests from curl, Wget, Python-urllib and libwww-perl user agents from 28 September 2022 to 10:32 UTC on 26 October 2026 [R045]. The blocked files include the two CRLs that carry the eight revocations, and HTTPS was no fallback because the certificates name HTTP CRL URLs [R045].
  • Clients that don't enforce CT are more exposed [R046]. CT-enforcing clients reject a leaf that was never logged, and non-browser clients such as curl usually don't enforce CT [R046]. One commenter counted 106,058 post-disclosure CT certificates that chain through the AAA cross-signs, a count nobody has replicated [R013].

Manual checks by area

Check every area you use, on every host and inside every image you ship. Each area below lists what to fix first. The checks page has one row per item, with a read-only command, the vulnerable and fixed versions and the AAA status (130 items, 303 sourced claims).

OS trust stores

  • Upgrade ca-certificates, rebuild images, then check the bundle file [R049]. Most distributions fixed this by removing emSign ECC Root CA - G3. Red Hat says its store cannot distrust single intermediates [R049]. The current rockylinux:8, :9 and :10 images still ship unfixed builds, and an upgrade installs the fixed build [R075, R074]. On Amazon Linux 2023 a plain dnf update ca-certificates leaves the unfixed build in place, and --releasever=latest installs the fix [R079].
  • Check for AAA Certificate Services as well as G3 [R104]. Removing G3 alone leaves the six Sectigo cross-signs trusted through AAA [R104]. Mozilla named AAA with the two G3 roots as candidates for removal [R048]. Unfixed Debian packages and the Ubuntu 22.04 and 24.04 release builds hold it [R053, R057]. Unfixed Red Hat-family packages hold it in the email and code-signing bundles [R070].
  • No fix yet on 2026-10-31 for Debian 11, openSUSE Leap 15.6, Tumbleweed on aarch64 or the Amazon Linux 2027 preview [R055, R091, R090, R081]. The newest Debian 11, Leap 15.6 and Tumbleweed aarch64 packages checked still trust the G3 root, and Debian 11's also trusts AAA [R055, R091, R090].
  • Check the code-signing bundle on Red Hat-family systems [R063, R072]. Fedora's first update and CentOS Stream 10's current build take G3 out of TLS trust but still trust emSign Root CS CA - G3 for code signing [R063, R072]. On RHEL only the .3 builds are advisory versions [R069].
  • Check /etc/ssl/cert.pem on every Mac [R098]. curl, git, python3, ruby, perl and openssl from macOS or the Command Line Tools trust that file and skip Apple's block and revocation checks [R098]. macOS 26.6.2 ships it with both roots, and its /usr/bin/curl completes HTTPS to hosts serving the compromised chains (checked 2026-10-31) [R099, R100]. Apple has named no fixed version [R098]. Homebrew's own bundle is fixed from 2026-09-25_1 [R093].
  • Keep CTL and Play services updates flowing on Windows and Android [R106, R107]. Windows blocks the eight keys through the Disallowed CTL and removed seven certificates on 26 October 2026, including one Microsoft calls an AAA Certificate Services cross-sign, whose thumbprint is the AAA root's [R106, R107]. Devices with automatic updates off or no internet access need the CTLs delivered another way [R108]. Microsoft says software that doesn't reliably check revocation or its disallowed list can still accept the compromised chains if it keeps or restores trust in the removed roots. The community tracker says apps with their own CA bundle need their own update [R109]. Android received its block through Google Play services on 19 October 2026 [R110].

Java

  • Check every JDK by fingerprint, then by subject [R119]. The same G3 root sits under four alias styles, and OpenJDK's alias names miss Temurin, Microsoft and Corretto's second copy [R119]. keytool -list prints SHA-256 fingerprints by default and -v adds the owner, so grep for both [R120, R121]. Each installation has its own cacerts, and a running JVM keeps the old store until it restarts [R126].
  • Fixed means G3 and AAA Certificate Services are both gone [R116, R117]. Sectigo's AAA root issued cross-signs for all eight compromised intermediates, and OpenJDK's second fix removes AAA along with G3, G1 and G2 [R116, R117]. Fixed Temurin keeps G1 on purpose, so a BAD ROOT: CN=emSign Root CA - G1,… line from OpenJDK's CheckCerts is expected there [R141, R125]. Fixed Corretto keeps emSign C1 and C3. A reply on Corretto's issue tracker says they don't chain to the compromised intermediates [R146].
  • Version numbers mislead across vendors [R114]. OpenJDK's advisory of 2026-10-24 lists 27.0.1.0.2, 25.0.5.0.2, 21.0.13.0.2, 17.0.21.0.2, 11.0.32.1, 8u504 and earlier as affected [R114]. The .0.2 builds from OpenJDK, Oracle and SapMachine still hold G3 and AAA, yet Temurin 27.0.1.0.2+1 is fixed [R127, R131, R154, R138]. Azul's fixed Zulu 21 subscriber build reports JDK 21.0.12.1, while the free 21.0.13 build still holds all four roots [R151, R152].
  • Several lines had no fix on 2026-10-31 [R139, R138]. Temurin 8 and 11 and the eclipse-temurin:27-jre tag still trust G3 [R139, R138]. The free Azul Zulu 21 build (disputed, see [R152]) and Microsoft Build of OpenJDK 21 and 25 still hold both G3 and AAA [R152, R153]. IBM names no fix for Semeru 8 or 11, and no fixed Oracle GraalVM build was found [R156, R132].
  • A JDK update leaves other Java stores alone [R157]. On Debian and Ubuntu, updating ca-certificates doesn't refresh /etc/ssl/certs/java/cacerts [R157]. Application truststores keep the roots until you remove them [R159]. Homebrew and FreeBSD OpenJDK carry their own store and need their own update [R150].
  • Only OpenJDK's second fix removes the roots [R116]. The first fix (JDK-8395142) blocked 16 certificates by fingerprint and kept the roots, and Sonatype reports that re-encoded copies got past it [R116]. The second (JDK-8395690) deletes four roots, and a fixed build's release note names JDK-8395690 and CVE-2026-114820 [R117, R118]. CVE-2026-91403 and CVE-2026-114820 are Oracle CVE records naming only Oracle products, and OpenJDK's advisories of 2026-10-21 and 2026-10-24 are separate documents [R113, R112, R115, R114].

Python

  • Upgrade pip to 26.2.2 in every environment [R167, R169]. pip vendors its own certifi and always trusts it, alongside the OS store from pip 24.2 on Python 3.10 and later, so a fixed OS store or certifi package leaves an older pip trusting G3 [R167, R169]. pip 26.2.2 is the first release whose vendored bundle leaves out G3 [R168]. Each venv has its own pip and a pyvenv.cfg at its root, so find them all and upgrade each one [R171].
  • Target certifi 2026.10.19 or later [R162]. GHSA-6q48-6c7w-9gw2 names 2026.10.18 as the first patched release, and the PSF recommends 2026.10.19 [R162]. 2026.10.18 removed every emSign root, including the unaffected G1, C1 and C3, and users reported broken connections [R161]. 2026.10.19 restores those three and still leaves out G3 [R162].
  • Check certifi from before 2019 too [R164]. The advisory range starts at 2019.03.09 and covers the G3 root only [R164]. Every certifi up to 2025.1.31, including 2018.11.29, holds Sectigo's AAA Certificate Services root, which cross-signed the compromised intermediates [R164].
  • Treat venvs made by Python 3.11 and 3.12 as affected [R178]. Current releases of these lines bundle pip 24.0 and 25.0.1, both holding G3 and the AAA root, and a reply on the PSF thread says security releases will not upgrade them [R178]. Python 3.15.2, 3.14.10, 3.13.18, 3.12.16 and 3.11.18 add an ssl block on the compromised keys that also catches the Sectigo cross-signs [R177]. Python 3.10 and older are end of life and get no fix [R179].
  • Update uv, Pixi and conda on their own [R172, R174]. uv 0.13.4 drops G3, and after uv 0.13.5, uv python upgrade replaces the managed 3.11 and newer interpreters [R172, R174]. Standalone Pixi needs 0.82.1 [R188]. conda needs its CA packages updated in every environment, because updating base leaves the others alone [R185]. Anaconda main has no fixed CA packages for osx-64, linux-ppc64le or linux-s390x [R187].
  • Fix requests, HTTPX and truststore at their source [R181, R182, R183]. requests and HTTPX use certifi, and truststore uses the OS store [R181, R182, R183]. REQUESTS_CA_BUNDLE replaces certifi for requests, and for pip it only adds CAs [R181, R169].

JavaScript runtimes

  • Upgrade Node.js to 22.23.4, 24.22.1 or 26.12.1 [R190]. Node compiles a Mozilla root snapshot into its binary, and only a Node upgrade changes it [R190]. These releases move to NSS 3.131.1, which drops G3, and reject chains with blocked public keys [R191, R192, R193, R194, R196]. The Node.js security release lists the CA change as a dependency update with no CVE [R197].
  • Move off Node.js 20 [R195]. It reached end of life on 2026-04-30 and has no fix [R195]. Its last release, 20.20.2, still bundles G3 [R195].
  • Do not rely on extra CA settings in Node.js [R198]. NODE_EXTRA_CA_CERTS, --use-system-ca and NODE_USE_SYSTEM_CA=1 add certificates to the bundled roots, and Node does not apply the system's distrust settings [R198].
  • Grep every Node binary for the G3 PEM line [R192, R193, R194]. Node stores its roots as PEM text, so the first base64 line of the G3 root appears in an affected binary and not in a fixed one [R192, R193, R194]. This grep was confirmed on Linux x64 builds only [R192].
  • Upgrade Bun to 1.4.4 and Deno to 2.9.8 [R201, R203]. Bun 1.4.3 and Deno 2.9.7 still hold G3 [R201, R203]. Deno 2.9.7 also holds the AAA root [R204]. Both store DER, so grep their binaries for the subject string emSign ECC Root CA - G3 [R201, R203].
  • Grep node_modules yourself [R200]. Electron's is the only npm advisory for this incident between 2026-10-17 and 2026-10-31, and no advisory names a package with a vendored bundle [R200]. An advisory search cannot prove that no package vendors the root, so run the node_modules grep in each project [R200].

Other languages

  • Go needs two checks [R205, R206, R207]. Go 1.26.10 and 1.27.3 add a key denylist to crypto/x509 for CVE-2026-111943, so rebuild every binary with one of them [R205, R206, R207]. govulncheck -mode binary flags binaries built with an older toolchain [R209]. golang.org/x/crypto/x509roots/fallback embeds its own roots and has no entry in the Go vulnerability database, so govulncheck misses an old pin [R210, R213]. Pin v0.0.0-20261020153306-9f2b28d4331a or later and rebuild [R211, R214].
  • Read every webpki-roots and webpki-root-certs entry in Cargo.lock [R216, R220]. 1.0.10 is the first fixed release of both [R216, R220]. 0.26.11 holds no roots and is safe only when the lock file also resolves the 1.x crate to 1.0.10 or later [R218, R220]. RUSTSEC-2026-0381 patches nothing else below 1.0.10, so 0.25.x and older have no fix [R216, R218]. The roots are compiled in, so rebuild after updating [R215].
  • A key block also stops the AAA cross-signs [R207, R223, R229]. Go 1.26.10 and 1.27.3, rustls-webpki 0.103.16 and composer/ca-bundle 1.6.0 and 1.4.16 match the eight compromised public keys, which Sectigo's cross-signs share [R207, R223, R229]. Composer 2.11.0 and 2.2.31 reject the intermediates even when the system store trusts their roots [R231]. webpki-roots, excon, castore, Hex certifi, Mozilla::CA and curl's cacert.pem fix by removing the G3 root [R217, R234, R244, R246, R240, R252]. Removing G3 alone leaves the cross-signs unblocked wherever the AAA root is trusted [R237].
  • Upgrade old pins to the first release after the incident [R219, R221, R230, R235, R242, R245, R247, R212]. These older releases bundle the AAA root as well as G3: webpki-roots and webpki-root-certs 0.26.9, composer/ca-bundle 1.5.6 and 1.4.7, excon 1.2.5, Mozilla::CA 20250202, castore 1.0.13, Hex certifi 2.14.0, the February 2025 Go fallback bundle, and everything before them [R219, R221, R230, R235, R242, R245, R247, R212].
  • httpclient has no fix [R236]. 2.9.0, the latest release, loads its own bundle by default [R236]. That bundle is Mozilla data from 2015 with the AAA root and no G3 root, so a check for G3 alone passes it [R236, R237].
  • PHP, RubyGems, Bundler, Erlang/OTP public_key, .NET and rustls-native-certs follow the OS store [R232, R233, R249, R250, R225]. Fix the OS store for them [R232, R233, R249, R250, R225]. rustls-native-certs ignores OS distrust settings, and .NET on Linux ignores the Disallowed store, so the root has to leave the store itself [R225, R251]. Dart and Flutter apps on Android and Windows ignore OS blocklists, so rebuild them with Dart 3.13.7 or Flutter 3.47.8 [R255, R256].

Browsers and apps

  • On default settings, browsers block the intermediates without an update, apart from a Firefox ESR 115 startup gap [R257, R259, R260, R264, R269]. Chrome blocks the keys through a CRLSet, which also covers the AAA cross-signs [R257]. Edge and WebView2 use their own CRLSet [R259]. Firefox uses OneCRL [R260]. Apple pushes trust data to Safari, Mail and its frameworks [R269].
  • Firefox ESR 115 has no released fix for new profiles [R264]. It loads OneCRL only after 20 idle seconds, and the fix is in an unreleased 115.45.0 [R264]. CVE-2026-113470 still lists 115.43.1 as fixed [R264]. A fork that drops OneCRL, such as GNU IceCat built from ESR 153.5.1, still trusted the intermediates [R265].
  • Check each Electron app's Electron version [R272]. Every Electron app needs its own release on a fixed Electron, and there is no app-side workaround [R272]. Read the Electron version from the bundle without running the app [R273, R274]. Fixed Electron releases still contain the G3 root, because the fix blocks the intermediates and leaves the root in place [R271]. Grepping the binary for the root's PEM line shows the bundled Node.js store, and it matches on fixed releases too [R275].
  • The community tracker disagrees with the vendors on four apps [R276, R277, R280, R283, R289]. VS Code desktop needs 1.144.0, since 1.143.1 changed only the remote server's Node.js [R276, R277]. Docker Desktop 4.96.3 is the first release whose notes drop the partial-fix warning, while the tracker lists 4.96.2 [R283]. certbot 5.9.0 and LibreOffice 26.8.2 shipped fixes that the tracker still lists as pending [R280, R289].
  • No fix yet for containerd, Podman, Godot, OpenHarmony or LibreOffice 26.2 [R281, R285, R287, R291, R290]. containerd merged the Go update on 20 October and has not released it [R281]. Podman has no stable release since 12 October [R285]. Godot's fix is on master only [R287]. OpenHarmony merged a change with no release [R291]. LibreOffice 26.2 waits on 26.2.7 [R290].
  • Look for the AAA root in app bundles too [R292, R293]. JBR b635.78 blocks all 16 certificates but its cacerts keeps the G3 and AAA roots, which b635.79 removes [R292, R293]. Godot 3 bundles AAA as well as the G3 root [R288].

Container images

  • Patching the host does not fix its containers [R294]. Each container trusts the bundle inside its image [R294]. Check the image and rebuild it [R295].

Automated checker

Source on GitHub

We provide a free check, vuln-check-emudhra-ca-compromise.pyz, that runs most of these checks against one machine in a single pass. It is a single Python zipapp that needs Python 3.9 or later and no packages, and it only reads files. Usage is in the repository README.

curl -fLO https://raw.githubusercontent.com/fieldse/emudhra-ca-compromise-check/v2.0.0/scripts/vuln-check-emudhra-ca-compromise.pyz
curl -fLO https://raw.githubusercontent.com/fieldse/emudhra-ca-compromise-check/v2.0.0/scripts/vuln-check-emudhra-ca-compromise.pyz.sha256
shasum -a 256 -c vuln-check-emudhra-ca-compromise.pyz.sha256
unzip -l vuln-check-emudhra-ca-compromise.pyz   # review before running
python3 -I -S vuln-check-emudhra-ca-compromise.pyz -r ~ -q                      # scan home, print problems only
python3 -I -S vuln-check-emudhra-ca-compromise.pyz -r ~/src -x ~/src/vendor -q  # skip one directory
python3 -I -S vuln-check-emudhra-ca-compromise.pyz --root=/opt --containers     # also probe local Podman images
python3 -I -S vuln-check-emudhra-ca-compromise.pyz -i registry/app:tag          # probe one local image
python3 -I -S vuln-check-emudhra-ca-compromise.pyz --help

-I -S keeps the scanned machine’s Python settings, site and .pth files out of the check, so always run it as python3 -I -S FILE. Run through its shebang, Python runs those hooks before the archive re-executes itself under -I -S. The archive is reproducible: the README shows how to rebuild it in a container under Python 3.9 or 3.13 and compare it byte for byte.

What it checks

  • Certificates and keys inside files. It looks in CA bundles, Java keystores (read directly, without keytool), and Node.js, Bun, Deno, Go and Rust binaries and Electron apps for emSign ECC Root CA - G3 and the compromised certificates. It matches each certificate and its public key in PEM or DER, so bundles without comments or labels are covered. It never runs a binary it judges.
  • Versions against the fixes on this page. It compares OS ca-certificates packages, Homebrew’s ca-certificates, runtimes (Node.js, Bun, Deno, Go, CPython), language packages (certifi, pip, uv, Electron, Rust, Composer, Ruby, Elixir and Perl bundles), Go and Rust binaries, Electron apps and ShipIt updater caches with the fixed releases.
  • Where it looks. -r sets each directory to scan, $HOME by default, and -x keeps it out of a directory. It also reads the OS bundle files, Homebrew’s bundle, the cacerts of each Java home, and programs in GOBIN, GOPATH/bin, ~/.cargo/bin, ~/.local/bin, /usr/local/bin and Homebrew’s bin. On macOS it always adds /Applications and Homebrew’s etc and Caskroom.
  • Images, opt in. -c also probes every local Podman image with networking off, and -i probes one image. The probe reads runtimes, Python packages, the ca-certificates package, CA bundles and Java keystores.
  • What it runs. For version answers it runs node, bun, deno, go, python3, uv and perl, and on macOS security, xcode-select and java_home, plus podman with -c. Each comes from PATH and is skipped when it sits in an excluded directory. On a machine whose PATH tools you don’t trust, scan from a clean account or pass -x for those directories.
FAIL
A bundle, keystore or binary still holds the G3 root or a compromised certificate, or a release is older than its fix.
WARN
Needs manual review. The AAA Certificate Services root is reported here [R040].
INFO
Context, such as a compromised key held as a blocklist entry or a certificate held as a rejected entry.
ERROR
A check that failed to run. The rest of the run continues.

The check exits 1 on any FAIL, 2 on a usage or environment error, and 0 otherwise. A WARN or ERROR alone exits 0. Pass --strict to exit 1 on those too when it gates CI. A stock Mac fails --strict on the AAA line for /etc/ssl/cert.pem until Apple updates that file [R099, R044].

Changes from the Bash script

The zipapp replaces the earlier Bash script. Its lines keep the same format, with these changes:

  • AAA is WARN. AAA Certificate Services was INFO and is now WARN, so --strict fails on it.
  • Exact Electron versions below the fix are FAIL. An exact "43.8.0" or "=43.8.0" in package.json can install only that version. Ranges (^, ~, N.x) below the fix stay WARN. A declared Electron line names the package.json path and opens its detail with pins V for an exact spec or range starts at V for a range.
  • rpm lines show the full VERSION-RELEASE. For example found 2025.2.80_v9.0.304-6.fc44, fixed 2026.2.92_v9.0.319-2, as rpm -q prints it.
  • The PEM count includes linked files. checked N PEM files counts each certificate a link in /etc/ssl/certs points to.
  • Old rustls-webpki in a binary with no affected root is WARN. A Rust binary whose rustls-webpki is below 0.103.16 lacks the key blocklist [R223], which matters only when it uses the system trust store. It is FAIL when the same binary holds the G3 root or a compromised intermediate, and WARN otherwise. uv 0.13.4 gets this WARN, and uv calls 0.13.4 fixed [R172].
  • ERROR lines. They can appear, and the SUMMARY line gains error=N.

What it doesn’t check yet

  • Electron apps on Windows. It reads macOS and Linux app layouts only. Fixed Electron binaries still contain the G3 root, because the fix blocks the intermediates and leaves the root in place [R271], so it judges apps by their Electron version [R273, R274].
  • Other apps. It has no version check for Firefox ESR 115, LibreOffice, Godot, Docker Desktop or Git for Windows. Bundles and keystores inside them are read only when they sit under a scan root.
  • Paths outside the scan roots. It misses JDKs that are not JAVA_HOME, the java on PATH or (on macOS) listed by java_home, Debian’s /etc/ssl/certs/java/cacerts when no JDK it reads links to it, and Go and Rust binaries outside the directories above. Add those directories with -r.
  • Some OS bundles. It doesn’t read the Red Hat-family code-signing and email bundles.
  • Packages without a version check. Composer itself, Ruby httpclient, Go’s x/crypto/x509roots/fallback in go.mod (it is checked inside Go binaries), conda, Pixi, uv-managed Python, CPython’s bundled pip, Dart and Flutter.
  • Platform stores. The macOS system keychain (reported as notes only), Windows trust lists, iOS, Android and Bottlerocket.
  • Inside images. The image probe doesn’t read Go or Rust binaries, Electron apps or lock files.

YARA rule

The repository also ships rules/yara/emudhra-ca.yar, which matches the G3 root and the compromised intermediates in any file, and rules/iocs.csv with all 16 compromised certificates. A hit for the compromised intermediates inside Deno 2.9.8 or later likely comes from Deno’s own distrust list, so judge Deno by its version and the root grep [R203].

If you can’t upgrade today

Take the compromised roots out of each store you can’t update yet, then upgrade when a fix ships. Remove AAA Certificate Services along with the G3 root, or the cross-signed path stays open [R040].

Any trust store

  • Remove both emSign ECC Root CA - G3 and AAA Certificate Services from any bundle you can't update [R040]. Removing G3 alone leaves the six Sectigo TLS cross-signs trusted through AAA [R040]. Mozilla advised distributions that cannot block the intermediates to consider removing AAA Certificate Services, emSign Root TLS CA - G3 and emSign ECC Root CA - G3 [R047].

OS trust stores

  • On macOS, remove the roots from /etc/ssl/cert.pem with Apple's interim steps [R104]. Apple's steps remove emSign ECC Root CA - G3 and optionally AAA Certificate Services, and removing G3 alone leaves the six Sectigo cross-signs trusted [R104]. A macOS update can replace the file, so re-check it after every update [R103]. The community tracker reports that 27.2 undid hand edits [R102].
  • On Bottlerocket 1.67.0 and earlier, apply AWS's settings.pki override [R085]. It sets trusted = false for emSign ECC Root CA - G3, C3 and C1 through apiclient apply until the node runs 1.68.0 [R085]. The override does not list AAA, which is not in the bundle 1.67.0 uses [R085, R084].
  • On disconnected Windows devices, deliver the updated CTLs another way [R108]. Without automatic updates or internet access the CTL Updater cannot fetch them [R108]. Microsoft gives 01dd5f6ca45c9d86 as the sequence number of the Disallowed CTL with the eMudhra block [R106].
  • On Android devices without the Play services block, restart first [R110]. A restart triggers the update [R110]. Failing that, the community tracker suggests disabling emSign ECC Root CA - G3 and AAA Certificate Services under Settings, Trusted credentials, System [R111].

Java

  • Delete G3 and AAA from each cacerts under the vendor's own alias [R123]. OpenJDK's stopgap is to back up the file, run keytool -delete -cacerts -alias "emsigneccrootcag3 [jdk]" for each of the four [jdk] aliases, ignore "does not exist" errors and restart running JVMs [R123]. Those names miss Temurin, Microsoft and Corretto's second copy, so run the fingerprint check with grep -B1 first to print each matching entry's alias [R119, R120]. On JDK 8, use -keystore "$JAVA_HOME/jre/lib/security/cacerts" in place of -cacerts [R122].
  • Rebuild the Debian and Ubuntu Java keystore [R158]. After updating ca-certificates, run rm -f /etc/ssl/certs/java/cacerts and then update-ca-certificates --fresh as root [R158]. A package update alone leaves the old keystore in place [R157].
  • Clean application truststores by hand [R159]. Run the same keytool -delete with -keystore and the file's password, because the default cacerts fix does not reach these files [R159].
  • Turn on revocation checking as a last resort [R124]. For TLS clients, OpenJDK suggests adding -Dcom.sun.net.ssl.checkRevocation=true -Dcom.sun.security.enableCRLDP=true to the java command line [R124]. For signed JARs on JDK 17 and later, it suggests jarsigner -verify -revcheck -strict on each JAR [R124].

Python and JavaScript runtimes

  • Bootstrap uv through the OS verifier [R175]. --system-certs, UV_SYSTEM_CERTS=true or system-certs = true in uv.toml make uv verify with the operating system instead of its bundled roots, for example UV_SYSTEM_CERTS=true uv self update [R175]. Fix the OS store first [R175].
  • Point uv at a fixed bundle [R175, R162]. SSL_CERT_FILE or SSL_CERT_DIR replaces uv's default certificate source entirely, so a fixed file such as the cacert.pem from certifi 2026.10.19 takes over [R175, R162].
  • Point requests at a fixed bundle [R181]. REQUESTS_CA_BUNDLE or CURL_CA_BUNDLE makes requests use that file instead of certifi [R181]. These settings do not help pip, which keeps its vendored roots and only adds the extra CAs [R169].
  • Upgrade pip inside each venv you cannot rebuild [R171, R176]. Each venv keeps its own pip, so upgrading the interpreter leaves existing venvs on their old pip [R171, R176]. Bring each one to pip 26.2.2 [R168].
  • Run Node.js with --use-openssl-ca on Linux once the distribution store is fixed [R199]. The flag replaces the bundled roots with OpenSSL's default store, which most Linux distributions maintain [R199]. This was not tested against a fixed store [R199].
  • Run Deno with DENO_TLS_CA_STORE=system once the OS store is fixed [R202]. The variable picks the system store, the bundled Mozilla roots or both, in order, and defaults to the bundled roots [R202]. Upgrade to 2.9.8 anyway, because which 2.9.7 code path loads the AAA root was not traced [R204].

Other languages

  • Point Go, curl, .NET on Linux and rustls-native-certs at curl's cacert-2026-10-20.pem with SSL_CERT_FILE [R252, R253]. That bundle holds neither the G3 root nor the AAA root [R252, R253]. All four read SSL_CERT_FILE [R208, R254, R250, R225]. The curl tool reads it, and CURL_CA_BUNDLE, only when it uses no native store, so a Schannel build on Windows ignores both [R254]. From Go 1.27, setting it on macOS or Windows turns off Go's platform verifier [R208].
  • For .NET on Linux, remove the roots from the OpenSSL bundle [R250, R251]. .NET ignores the Disallowed store when it builds chains on Linux [R251].
  • Keep a fixed CA bundle in Go containers and leave GODEBUG=x509usefallbackroots=1 unset [R210]. Go then uses the system roots and never the embedded fallback bundle [R210].
  • Give Perl a fixed, usable system store [R243]. IO::Socket::SSL clients such as LWP and HTTP::Tiny load Mozilla::CA only when no usable system store is found [R243].

Apps and container images

  • Firefox ESR 115: leave a new profile idle for 20 seconds before first use [R264]. ESR 115 loads OneCRL only after 20 seconds without input [R264]. Once it loaded, the tracker's test page showed none of the 14 chains trusted [R264].
  • Upgrade ca-certificates in the image build when the base image lags [R300]. On rockylinux:8, :9 and :10, dnf upgrade ca-certificates installs the fixed builds [R300]. On amazonlinux:2023, add --releasever=latest to the update, or it keeps the unfixed build [R301].
  • Move distroless images to Debian 13 [R299]. static-debian13 ships the fixed ca-certificates 20250419+deb13u1 [R299]. The Debian 12 images had no fix on 2026-10-31 [R298].
  • Update the podman machine VM's own ca-certificates [R286]. The VM keeps a store separate from the host, and the community tracker gives Fedora 2026.2.92_v9.0.319 as fixed [R286].
